Helpy — Calendar tab

Three ways to build it, drawn with the real Sky / Midnight tokens, Inter, and the flat-surface rules from DESIGN.md. Same brief in all three: a third tab beside Lists and Planning, a list picker, and tasks you drag onto time. They differ in how precise the time is and what that writes back to Apple Reminders.

A Week grid + unscheduled rail

The Google Calendar shape. Left rail is everything in the list that has no time yet, each card carrying its estimate. Drag one onto a slot and it becomes a block whose height is its estimate; drag the bottom edge to change the estimate. The day headers count how much you have allocated, and go warm when you have booked more than the day holds.

Writes: due date with a time on the reminder (syncs to iPhone), and the estimate in EstimateStore. A block is the same task the board shows — moving it here moves it there.

B One day, big blocks

Same idea, one day at a time. Blocks are wide enough to carry the real task card — checkbox, list, estimate versus time actually spent — so this doubles as the screen you work from, not just the one you plan on. The meter at the top is the day's capacity: booked, spent, still free.

Writes: identical to A. The week view can still exist as a zoom-out; this is about which one you land on.

C Week capacity board — no clock

The half-step. Seven day columns, no hour ruler: you drop a task on a day, not at 10:15, and the block's height is still its estimate so the column fills up in front of you. The header bar is the honest bit — "6h 15m of 6h" turns red and tells you the day is oversold before you live it.

Writes: only a due date, day components — exactly what the board already writes today, so no times and no alarms land in Apple Reminders. Cheapest to build, and it never fights the Backlog / This week / Today columns.
